# Month names

I intended the month names mainly to describe what is happening through the solar year: the Sun's changing position, the lengthening and shortening of daylight, and the movement through the seasons. Ré Anann is the exception, a dedication attached to the seasonal Black Moon.

These names don't need to match my [Natural Tuning](natural-tuning.md) framework. That describes how I reflect and act within the year. The names should help me recognize where I am in the cycle.

These are modern calendar names, using Irish words and a historical divine name. I've revised three places in the sequence to make their seasonal meaning clearer. [Detailed language and naming research](language-and-naming-research.md) records the evidence behind the choices.

## The current vocabulary

Seasonal descriptions follow my Northern Hemisphere, temperate-season perspective. They describe the place of a lunar month within the year, rather than a condition that begins precisely at its new moon.

| Name | Meaning | What I intend it to evoke |
|---|---|---|
| **Idir-Ré** | [Interlunar period](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/idir-r%C3%A9). I use “between-time” as a poetic interpretation. | The month containing the vernal equinox, spanning the change of solar year. |
| **Éirí** | [Rising](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/%C3%A9ir%C3%AD). | The Sun's higher daily path as the year moves toward summer. |
| **Neartú** | [Strengthening](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/neart%C3%BA). | The growing presence of daylight before the summer-solstice month. |
| **Grian Thuaidh** | Northern Sun; *grian* with the adjective *thuaidh*. | The month containing the Sun's northernmost position relative to the celestial equator, at the summer solstice. |
| **Iompú** | [Turning](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/iomp%C3%BA). | The changed direction of the solar cycle after the summer-solstice month. |
| **Giorrú** | [Shortening](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/giorr%C3%BA). | The shortening days approaching the autumnal-equinox month. |
| **Cothromú** | [Equalization, balancing](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/cothrom%C3%BA). | The month containing the autumnal equinox, as the Sun crosses the celestial equator into the southern sky. |
| **Ísliú** | [Lowering, descent](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/%C3%ADsli%C3%BA). | The Sun's lower daily path after the autumnal-equinox month. |
| **Doimhniú** | [Deepening](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/doimhnigh). | The deepening of the darker part of the year before the winter-solstice month. |
| **Grian Theas** | Southern Sun; *grian* with the adjective *theas*. | The month containing the Sun's southernmost position relative to the celestial equator, at the winter solstice. |
| **Filleadh** | [Return](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/filleadh). | The return toward longer days after the winter-solstice month. |
| **Síneadh** | [Lengthening, extension](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/s%C3%ADneadh). | The lengthening days approaching Idir-Ré. |
| **Ré Anann** | Time or Moon of Anu; [evidence for the wording](language-and-naming-research.md#ré-anann). | A dedication in the month whose day 1 contains the third new moon of a season containing four. |

The north/south descriptions concern the Sun's position relative to the celestial equator, not its compass bearing from a particular observer. The [research notes](language-and-naming-research.md#grian-thuaidh-and-grian-theas) explain the grammar and the distinction from ordinary Irish terms for the solstices.

## Why I changed these names

**Cothromú and Réiteach.** “Balancing” suggests the equinox more readily than “resolution” does. The original explanation treated Cothromú as movement toward balance and Réiteach as its arrival, but that distinction isn't obvious from the words themselves.

I now use **Iompú → Giorrú → Cothromú**: turning, shortening, balancing. Giorrú takes the earlier place of Cothromú, and Cothromú replaces Réiteach in the autumnal-equinox month. “Balancing” is my image of that crossing; an equinox doesn't generally bring exactly twelve hours each of daylight and darkness. [Language evidence and alternatives](language-and-naming-research.md#cothromú-réiteach-and-the-approach-to-the-equinox).

**Aisling and Síneadh.** Aisling means a vision or dream. I've replaced it with **Síneadh**, lengthening, to describe the longer days approaching Idir-Ré. It pairs with Giorrú on the other side of the year. The change reflects what I want the names to communicate. [Evidence for Síneadh](language-and-naming-research.md#why-síneadh-replaces-aisling).

## Assigning names

1. Set each month's beginning at the sunset opening the day that contains its new moon. Identify the months containing the vernal equinox, summer solstice, autumnal equinox, and winter solstice. Assign Idir-Ré, Grian Thuaidh, Cothromú, and Grian Theas respectively.
2. Count the new-moon instants in each astronomical season. Include an event at its opening boundary and exclude one at its closing boundary. If there are four, the month whose day 1 contains the third is Ré Anann.
3. Between each pair of quarter-point months, set aside any Ré Anann. If two unnamed lunations remain, assign the two ordinary names in the table, in order. Otherwise, retain their new-moon dates as identifiers until the naming convention covers that arrangement.

This describes the current tables. A change of name leaves the astronomical events intact. In the 2025–2026 year, Ré Anann is associated with the August new moon; in 2026–2027 and 2027–2028 it is absent. See the [Black Moon rule and example](calendar-rules.md#black-moon-and-ré-anann).

Quarter-point names follow the local dated months. If a quarter point and a new moon fall within the same sunset-to-sunset day, that day already belongs to the new month, even when the quarter point comes first. The Black Moon count still uses the exact new-moon instants and astronomical season boundaries.

For the two parts of Idir-Ré, the everyday instructions use **before equinox** and **after equinox**. *Críoch* and *Tús* can also serve as separate ending and beginning labels; their meanings and the older compound wording are covered in the [research notes](language-and-naming-research.md#the-two-parts-of-idir-ré).
